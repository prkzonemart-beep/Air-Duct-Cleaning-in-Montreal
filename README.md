The Montreal Climate Problem Nobody Mentions
There’s a reason Montreal homes accumulate duct buildup faster than cities with milder climates.

Your furnace works harder here than almost anywhere else in Canada. Your AC puts in serious hours through July and August. Between the two, your duct system is in near-constant operation — and every hour it runs, it’s circulating whatever has settled inside your ductwork through every room in your home.

In an older Montreal home — a Plateau triplex, a NDG semi-detached, a Rosemont flat built in the 50s or 60s — that buildup isn’t just dust. It’s decades of accumulated debris, old insulation particles, pet dander, pollen from Montreal’s notoriously brutal allergy seasons, and in some cases mold that’s been quietly growing in ducts that have never seen a professional cleaning.

Your family is breathing all of it. Every single day.

What Dirty Ducts Are Actually Costing Montreal Homeowners
Most people don’t connect the dots until after a cleaning. But dirty ductwork has real, measurable costs that show up in your home every month:

Your heating and cooling bills are higher than they need to be. When ducts are clogged with buildup, your system has to work harder to push air through. In a Montreal winter where your furnace is running for months straight, that extra strain adds up significantly on your Hydro-Québec bill.

Someone in your home might be suffering unnecessarily. Montreal’s allergy season is brutal on its own. If a family member deals with persistent indoor allergies, frequent headaches, or a cough that never quite goes away, dirty ducts recirculating allergens and irritants through your home could be a major contributing factor.

That musty smell isn’t seasonal. A lot of Montreal homeowners chalk up that stale, musty odour from the vents to the building being old. Sometimes that’s true. But often it’s mold or years of settled biological debris inside the duct system. Air fresheners mask it. A proper cleaning eliminates it.

Your HVAC equipment is aging faster than it should. Montreal’s climate already pushes heating and cooling systems hard. Add restricted airflow from dirty ducts and you’re significantly shortening the lifespan of equipment that costs thousands to replace.

Montreal’s Older Housing Stock Makes This More Urgent
This is worth talking about specifically for Montreal, because the city’s housing stock is genuinely different from most Canadian cities.

A huge portion of Montreal homes are older — many built pre-1970, some much earlier. These buildings were constructed with duct systems never designed to go decades without professional cleaning. Many have been through multiple owners, multiple renovations, and in some cases the ductwork has never once been properly serviced.

If you live in a triplex, a row house, or any pre-1980 Montreal property and you don’t know when the ducts were last cleaned — assume they haven’t been. The buildup in older Montreal ductwork can be substantial, and the difference in air quality after a professional cleaning is often dramatic.

How Often Should Montreal Homeowners Clean Their Ducts?
NADCA recommends professional duct cleaning every 3 to 5 years for most homes. But Montreal’s climate and housing conditions often push that closer to every 2 to 3 years. Here’s a simple guide:

Every 3 to 5 years — standard home, no pets, no allergy sufferers
Every 2 to 3 years — home with dogs or cats
Every 2 to 3 years — allergy or asthma sufferers in the household
Every 1 to 2 years — older pre-1970 Montreal property
Immediately — post-renovation or recent construction work nearby
Before moving in — any newly purchased older Montreal home
If you’ve just bought a property and you’re not sure about the duct history — get it done before you settle in. It’s one of those things you’ll be glad you did.
